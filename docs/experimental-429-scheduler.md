# Experimental 429 scheduler: safety model and limits

**Manual opt-in experiment only; do not use in production.** `Experimental429Scheduler` and `CooldownCoordinator` are isolated prototypes, not integrated into routing, admission, application startup or provider handling. Nothing starts them automatically. They are not an admission fence. The application-database prototype requires its expected experimental schema and a previously initialized database; do not treat its fixture schema as a production migration. Independent review and integration work would be needed before any production consideration.

## Ownership and lifecycle

- A caller explicitly constructs a scheduler with a **fresh, exclusively owned** coordinator and injected timers, then calls `start()`. Repeated `start()` calls return the same completion promise and do not start another loop. `stop()` is idempotent and returns that same promise; stopping before starting prevents any tick. The coordinator rejects overlapping ticks and cannot be reused after it has been stopped. A constructor that rejects invalid scheduler options does not take ownership or stop the supplied coordinator.
- Ticks are serial: the first is attempted without an interval delay, and the next timer is scheduled only after the previous tick settles successfully. At shutdown the scheduler wakes its waiting loop, calls coordinator `stop()` once, and waits for both the loop and coordinator stop to settle. A late tick failure remains visible. One failure is propagated; multiple loop, timer-cancellation or stop failures are reported in an `AggregateError`.
- The database owner must keep the same initialized, open, autocommit shared database available throughout scheduler operation and shutdown, await the scheduler's completion (including rejection), and only then close or replace the database. Neither scheduler nor coordinator owns or closes the shared database. Coordinator stop requests cancellation of active work and clears in-memory observations, but cannot preempt synchronous or stuck work. Closing or replacing the database during a tick is not a supported shutdown procedure.
- On a timer cancellation exception, the scheduler records the exception and wakes its loop anyway, but cannot guarantee that the injected timer was actually cancelled. The timer implementation may leave a callback pending or keep resources alive after completion. A test where `clearTimeout` removes the timer *before* throwing does not establish cancellation when it throws first.

## Failure, retry and restart

Every tick error is terminal, including real or apparent SQLite `BUSY`/`LOCKED` errors; no scheduler retry or special error-code classification exists. `backoffMs` and `maxBackoffMs` are validated reserved options and have no effect. Clock and timer setup failures also terminate the loop and trigger shutdown. Observe the completion promise: failure is not a successful run.

**Decision and rationale (2026-11-02):** An error's `code`, including `SQLITE_BUSY`, does not authenticate its origin: a coordinator, callback, timer, or other injected dependency can produce the same code. Retrying on that basis could conceal a different failure or replay work after an uncertain transaction outcome. The conservative choice is therefore to terminate and surface the failure, sacrificing availability rather than assuming a tick is safe to repeat. An indiscriminate retry or a code-based busy/locked allowlist was considered and rejected; manual recovery with a fresh scheduler and coordinator remains possible after the failure is understood and shutdown completes.

A future retry design requires a trusted boundary that authenticates the error as originating from the specific SQLite lock-acquisition operation, distinguishes acquisition failure from callback execution and uncertain commit, and proves no side effects or ambiguous transaction outcome can be replayed. It also needs explicitly bounded, cancellation-aware backoff, a shutdown/lifetime contract, and isolated contention, forged-code, callback/commit-failure and shutdown tests plus independent review. These are prerequisites for considering a change, not existing guarantees or approval to integrate it.

A stopped or failed scheduler cannot be restarted. Recovery requires a new scheduler **and a fresh coordinator**, after handling the error and completing shutdown. Stopping discards observations and cursor state. On rediscovery, each surviving finite restriction needs a fresh full local monotonic observation; time elapsed before restart is not credited. Manual coordinator tests that retry a busy tick on the same still-running coordinator do not imply scheduler retry.

## What is bounded

`maxAttempts` caps the number of ticks started by one scheduler run; `lifetimeMs` is checked before a tick and after it settles. Neither bounds a tick's work, shutdown time or wall-clock lifetime: synchronous, slow or stuck tick/stop operations cannot be preempted, and timer delays can overshoot. The scheduler checks its injected clock is finite, nonnegative and nondecreasing; the coordinator separately requires a nonnegative, nondecreasing bigint monotonic clock. A coordinator clock fault clears all retained observations and its cursor; already committed deletions are not rolled back.

Coordinator `capacity` bounds retained observations, and `pageSize` bounds candidates returned per page, not total database work. Schema/state validation scans restriction rows on each tick and stopwatch check; full-table work and elapsed time are unbounded by these settings. An interval, attempt cap, capacity or page size is not a resource or latency guarantee.

## Conditional safety and progress

For a finite-duration row, a stopwatch begins observing the committed generation locally. It waits the full duration and, under a writer transaction, rechecks the database, generation, duration and clock before conditional deletion. A changed generation or duration requires a new observation; indefinite restrictions are not automatically expired. This depends on an intact expected schema, valid data, trusted generation writers and a trustworthy monotonic clock; it is not a guarantee against arbitrary corruption, untrusted writers or rollback of the database/VM state.

The coordinator cycles through finite candidates with a cursor and limited retained observations. Eventual progress is conditional on a stable finite set, enough repeated successful ticks and advancing valid time, available database access, and unchanged restrictions long enough to complete observation. Continuous growth, repeated errors/restarts, stopped processes, finite scheduler attempt/lifetime limits, or permanently busy/stuck work provide no eventual-expiry guarantee. Expiry is not a promise that a real provider will accept a request.

## Evidence boundary

The scheduler tests exercise injected timers and stub coordinators, including serial operation, terminal busy-like errors, shutdown and clock/timer failures. Coordinator tests use synthetic local database fixtures and injected clocks, with a limited real-monotonic-time check. These tests do not prove behavior with real providers or across an actual OS reboot, nor establish production safety or bounded performance. See `src/db/experimental-429-scheduler.ts`, `src/db/experimental-429-coordinator.ts`, `src/db/experimental-app-stopwatch.ts`, `tests/experimental-429-scheduler.test.mjs` and `tests/experimental-429-coordinator.test.mjs` for the implementation and scope of evidence.
