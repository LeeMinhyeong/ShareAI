# ADR 0008: New-request account exclusion after upstream 429

**Status: user-approved product decision; partially implemented, not approved for production.** **2026-11-09:** per [ADR 0016](0016-phase4-scope-freeze.md), the indefinite OPEN rule is under revision for attempts the router itself aborted (client cancel or router timeout); reproduce first, then amend this ADR. Other uncertain outcomes keep the conservative rule. This supersedes ADR 0006's proposed global-blocking default and ADR 0005's broader-scope default for new logical requests, not their observations about uncertainty or the existing 401 path.

**2026-11-01 decision on expiry:** The user chose automatic cooldown expiry rather than indefinite per-account blocking requiring manual recovery. The intended rule is to wait at least the applicable restriction duration before returning the account to eligibility; another eligible account may serve a *new* request during that wait. A local clock jump must not make the wait shorter. The implementation and feasibility of safe automatic timing across process restart, operating-system restart, and migration remain under investigation; until independently reviewed and tested, this decision is not an operational guarantee. Uncertain or missing evidence may still require conservative blocking, and an unresolved OPEN is not automatically cleared by the expiry decision. The current `Date.now()`-based cooldown can expire early after a wall-clock jump and is not production safe.

## Decision

**2026-11-09 amendment — router-initiated abort (user-approved, ADR 0016 item 4):** When the router itself aborts a dispatched attempt — the downstream client disconnected or the router's own inference timer fired — and the attempt was actually dispatched, the exact owned OPEN is deleted and, in the same writer transaction, a finite 60-second v2 restriction is armed for that `(kind, account)`. The account then returns through the existing monotonic-observation expiry; a restart restarts the full wait. This applies whether the abort happened before or after response headers. Reproduced before the change: a client disconnect or timer abort left the OPEN indefinitely, so one cancellation removed the account until offline recovery. Accepted risks: the provider may still be processing for up to and beyond 60 s, so a later request on the same account can overlap; a 429 that was about to arrive before headers is not observed and may be hit once more after the wait, at which point the normal 429 restriction applies. Aborts before dispatch keep their existing exact-claim release. Every other uncertain outcome — provider network failure after dispatch, truncated or contradictory streams, settlement or commit uncertainty, process crash — keeps the indefinite OPEN below; if the conversion's own commit is uncertain the OPEN is retained. This is a local availability policy, not evidence that the provider stopped work or that the limit scope is per account.

When an upstream 429 is observed, exclude the affected stable internal account identity from **new logical requests** for a durable, process-shared cooldown. Other otherwise eligible accounts may be selected for a new request, even when the 429's actual upstream scope is unknown. An explicitly pinned SIWC account remains pinned: never substitute another account. A dispatched request receiving 429 is not automatically retried on the same or another account. Existing separately implemented 401 refresh/reinvoke remains an unresolved exception, not an authorization for 429 retry.

This is a product availability choice, not a conclusion that a limit is account-local, an assurance that another account is independent, or permission under provider terms. Multiple credentials may share an organization, plan, provider or gateway limit; further requests may violate provider restrictions and incur account enforcement. The user expressly accepted the uncertainty for new requests. Provider terms and SIWC sharing authorization remain unverified; operators must check them before use. In particular the SIWC recovery documentation cited by ADR 0005 warns against silently changing billing paths following plan-usage errors; this decision does not override provider rules.

## Alternatives

* ADR 0006's conservative global/domain restriction: fewer shared-limit risks but materially reduces availability; user did not choose it.
* Process-local account backoff: inadequate because restarts and other processes bypass it.
* Same-request failover: rejected because of replay, duplicate execution/billing and provider-policy risk.
* No timed exclusion or silent logout/re-auth reset: rejected because an affected account would immediately be reused.

## Intended engineering contract

Use distinct `(kind, stable account id)` namespaces for ordinary and SIWC accounts, without storing tokens, prompts or headers. A 429 must be durably recorded before returning its error when possible, independent of client cancellation; an uncertain or failed commit cannot be reported as a confirmed durable restriction. A late 429 still extends the stable account's restriction after credential replacement, while a stale success never clears it or mutates newer credential status. Logout, refresh or reauthentication must not silently clear it. Selection is advisory; the existing writer-serialized dispatch gates must check the durable restriction immediately before starting network I/O, preserving SIWC ACL/pinning and ordinary revision checks. Never initiate another physical request for a 429 within that logical request. Ordinary accounts additionally use one durable OPEN attempt per stable account, committed before dispatch and without expiry. A crash or uncertain response leaves that account blocked, including across restart, credential changes, and deletion/reinsertion of the same identity. There is no implemented manual recovery workflow: preserve the evidence and do not delete OPEN rows to restore availability. This ordinary-account serialization does not assert cross-account serialization or equivalent SIWC protection.

The intended recovery design distinguishes absent from unusable hints. An absent `Retry-After` uses the existing five-second minimum. A valid delta-seconds value gives the full duration, subject to that minimum. A valid absolute date gives a finite duration only relative to a trustworthy, valid provider `Date`, with a full second of precision allowance; absent or untrustworthy provider dates, malformed hints, and durations outside the safe representation are unknown and must remain blocked until manual recovery. Re-arming starts a new generation and retains the longer full duration; if either duration is unknown, the result remains unknown. A restarted observer waits the full duration again, and forward wall-clock changes do not shorten it. This is the intended design, not a claim that current code implements it. Expiry is a local routing policy, not proof that the provider-wide limit has expired or that further use is permitted. No 429 restriction is inferred from a network error. No provider payload or secret is recorded.

The durable schema must be provisioned with a strict, conflict-detecting **offline** migration. The operator must stop all old routers and drain their in-flight work, verify a consistent backup, deploy only compatible new binaries, and separately approve the offline migration with provenance attestation. Existing cooldown rows, including expired and orphaned rows, must be preserved and carried forward as indefinite restrictions because their original full wait cannot be reconstructed; preserve the original values and all OPEN rows. Fresh pristine databases may provision the new schema, but an existing database must not be upgraded automatically. Mixed versions are unsupported: the boundary is operator-enforced, not a technical fence, and an old binary can bypass it if restarted. All participants need the same SQLite file and a supported single-host locking/storage boundary; this is not distributed multi-host consensus.

**Historical rollout assessment (superseded where inconsistent with README and `docs/offline-open-upgrade.md`):** At this earlier assessment, initialization created the cooldown table automatically if absent and no offline production upgrade existed. A later experimental offline upgrader covers only narrowly recognized synthetic baselines and has not been approved for production. The ordinary OPEN table and a dedicated singleton version marker are provisioned atomically only on a genuinely empty database. A missing table with a marker, an unmarked existing table, or any nonempty unmarked database is rejected without silently discarding evidence. In particular even a seemingly legacy database cannot prove in-band that both objects were never created and subsequently removed; there is no production-approved offline path to upgrade it. Stop all old processes, make and verify a consistent SQLite backup, and require separately reviewed offline provenance and migration before any rollout; do not point this code at production. Incompatible schemas are rejected rather than repaired. The marker is not tamper-proof: a DB owner can alter rows, remove both objects or replace the database, and an old binary can bypass the gate. These actions are outside this safety boundary; do not run mixed binaries.

## Verification and rollout

Before enabling, run red-first tests against authenticated real HTTP on two independent router processes sharing an isolated file-backed DB and a synthetic local provider: A receives 429 exactly once, a subsequent request on the other process chooses B, A is blocked until deadline, restart preserves the restriction, 401/cancellation never triggers 429 failover, unauthorized and explicitly pinned SIWC requests cannot switch, and stale result/credential races, logout/reauth, concurrent dispatch and failed or uncertain persistence are covered. Also test strict migration conflicts and non-429 behavior, then independently review all network initiation sites and provider policy. A scratch mock is not production or provider compliance evidence. Until these checks pass, this ADR records intent only, not a shipped safety guarantee.
